LIRR · FARE CHANGE OF JAN 4 2026 --:--

AFTER THE TEN-TRIP

On January 4, 2026 the Long Island Rail Road raised fares, retired the ten-trip ticket and swapped round trips for a Day Pass. Ask a question and a small team of AI researchers digs through 17 million rows of ticket sales, daily ridership, train crowding counts and both fare charts to find out what riders bought instead, and what they pay now.

Researchers on duty Data through Sep 30 2026 How it works ↓

Who it's for

LIRR riders wondering what to buy now that the ten-trip is gone, transit advocates and reporters checking the MTA's numbers, and anyone curious how a fare change ripples through a commuter railroad.

How to use it

  1. Ask in plain English, or tap an example.
  2. Watch the Lead plan, 2–4 Researchers query the data in parallel, and the Writer report.
  3. Tap any citation like q_1a2b3c4d to see the exact SQL and rows behind a number.

Answers take about 20–60 seconds. Vague questions get a clarifying question back.

What it can't tell you

  • Ticket counts, not revenue: spending is an estimate (tickets × fare chart prices).
  • A ten-trip counts as 10 tickets in the MTA data, and round trips mostly count per leg.
  • Prices from the Aug 2023 and Jan 2026 charts only; crowding covers M7/M9 electric trains only.
  • Sales and crowding cover Jan 2025 – Sep 2026.

Under the hood

A Lead agent plans and reviews, Researchers write their own SQL against DuckDB in fresh contexts, and a Writer cites every number. Code, not the model, checks that each citation is a query that ran and that each number appears in its rows.
